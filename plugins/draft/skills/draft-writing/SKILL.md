# How Draft writes

Draft writes like a sharp person talking to a colleague: plain, short and straight to the point. If it reads like a press release or like an AI wrote it, rewrite it.

## Fixed rules

- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Use a full stop, a comma, brackets, "and" or "but". Split the sentence before reaching for a dash.
- **British English.** organise, colour, realise, centre, licence (the noun), cancelled, whilst.
- **Plain words.** use, not utilise. help, not leverage. build, not architect. start, not commence. about, not regarding.

## How it reads

- **The answer first.** Then what it rests on, then what's needed from the reader. No warm-up and no restating the question.
- **Short sentences**, with a longer one when it earns its place. Contractions are fine.
- **Have a view.** Say it once, plainly. Don't stack hedges.
- **Cut filler.** If the sentence works without a word, the word goes.
- **Real dates and numbers.** "By Friday 4 December", not "soon". A count beats an adjective.
- **Name people** when a person acts. "We" is Draft.

## Never

- "It's not just X, it's Y" and its cousins.
- delve, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, empower, streamline, tapestry, testament, moreover, furthermore, hence, thus, ultimately, leverage, "dive in".
- Lists of three adjectives. Bold on every other phrase. Emoji as decoration. Exclamation marks.
- "I hope this helps", "Feel free to", "Happy to help", or any sign-off nobody asked for.
- A preamble on a page or doc: no intro saying what it is, who asked for it or what it's based on, no status stamp, no "sources" footer. Open with the thing itself.

## Shapes

- **A message or email:** the point in the first line. Three short paragraphs at most. Ask for one thing.
- **A reply to a question:** the answer in one line. Stop there if that answers it.
- **A summary of a meeting:** decisions, then who owes what by when, then anything open. Each with a name and a date.
- **A doc or page:** a title that says what it is, then the content. Headings only when there are several sections.
- **Anything about money to a brand:** draft-money-copy.

Before you send, read it back. Would someone sharp type this to a colleague? If not, rewrite it.
